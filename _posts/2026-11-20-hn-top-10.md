## Today's Top 10 on Hacker News

### 1. [Frostbite: A new CPU architecture discovered, designed and verified by AI](https://architectlabs.com/blog/frostbite)
⭐ 143 💬 93 👤 shioyama
[🔗 Discuss on HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50572503)

Frostbite is a general-purpose CPU architecture created with AI assistance, using a new instruction set based on interaction nets to make program dependencies explicit and local. The AI system iterated across compiler, ISA, architecture, and hardware verification, producing a design that achieved about 4.6 times the performance per watt of a modern superscalar core on a set of workloads. The result suggests AI can help discover novel chip designs that may overcome long-standing CPU performance stagnation.

### 2. [Carnival Cruises emits ten times more sulphur oxide than all of Europe's cars](https://ecohustler.com/article/one-corporation-to-pollute-them-all/)
⭐ 115 💬 59 👤 sammatilda
[🔗 Discuss on HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50572116)

A new analysis finds that Carnival Corporation, the world's largest cruise operator, emitted nearly ten times more sulphur oxide around European coasts than all 260 million European cars combined, with Royal Caribbean also far exceeding the car fleet. The report highlights heavy pollution in tourist-heavy countries such as Spain, Italy, Greece, France and Norway, and says cruise ships' nitrogen oxide emissions also significantly affect port cities. It urges Europe to adopt zero-emission port standards, expand emission control areas, and regulate nitrogen oxide emissions from existing ships.

### 10. [µ-Wire – USB on an ATtiny 10](https://cpldcpu.com/2014/03/19/%c2%b5-wire-usb-on-an-attiny-10/)
⭐ 50 💬 1 👤 mofosyne
[🔗 Discuss on HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50545818)

The article describes a project that turns the tiny ATtiny10 microcontroller into a USB device for controlling a WS2812 RGB LED. It implements a simplified little-wire protocol while remaining compatible with existing host programs, despite the chip’s limited 1 KB flash and 32 bytes of SRAM. The key challenge was reducing the V-USB stack to its bare essentials and using interrupt-free, timing-based processing to fit within the device’s constraints.
