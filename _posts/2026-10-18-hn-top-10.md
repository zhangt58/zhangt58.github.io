### 6. [Teal – A statically-typed dialect of Lua](https://teal-language.org/)
⭐ 40 💬 9 👤 Lyngbakr
[🔗 Discuss on HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50124730)

Teal is a statically typed dialect of Lua that adds type annotations such as arrays, maps, records, interfaces, union types, and generics. It compiles .tl source files into standard Lua, aiming to provide TypeScript-like safety while preserving Lua's minimalism and embeddability. The project includes a compiler, build tool, editor integrations, and documentation for developing larger Teal projects.

### 7. [Lean now ships with verified kernels](https://leodemoura.github.io/blog/2026-10-15-lean-now-ships-with-verified-kernels/)
⭐ 144 💬 48 👤 matt_d
[🔗 Discuss on HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50114758)

Lean now includes a lake check command that rechecks Lean developments using external proof-checking kernels, including the official kernel and several independent implementations. Among them is con-leche, a Lean-written kernel with a formal consistency proof, and con-ron, a Rust version verified to refine con-leche's checking algorithm. Together these tools let users detect kernel soundness bugs by cross-checking proofs with multiple verified or independent kernels.

### 8. [It's time to talk about TEEs](https://alinush.github.io//tee)
⭐ 3 💬 0 👤 joshcsimmons
[🔗 Discuss on HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50139658)

The article surveys attacks on trusted execution environments, including memory-safety bugs in enclave code, remote attestation forgery, and physical or microarchitectural vulnerabilities that can undermine Intel SGX, AMD SEV, and related platforms. It argues that TEE security claims are hard to verify and that side channels, complex CPU designs, and platform-level flaws make deployment risky. The author urges developers to reconsider whether to use TEE-based applications and, if they do, to adopt stricter verification and threat-model controls.
