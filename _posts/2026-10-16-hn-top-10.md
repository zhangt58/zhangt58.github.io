### 1. [Yet another way to invoke functions without parentheses](https://blog.ikaes.de/yet-another-way-to-invoke-functions-without-parentheses/)
⭐ 30 💬 8 👤 aragonite
[🔗 Discuss on HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50077225)

The article describes a JavaScript technique for invoking arbitrary functions without parentheses by overriding Error.prepareStackTrace so that accessing an error’s stack creates a function from the stack trace. It then makes the stack array stringify into attacker-controlled code by replacing Array.prototype.toString or using prototype pollution, and finally triggers execution through valueOf or string concatenation.

### 5. [The Economic Complexity of the Western Roman Empire](https://www.michelecoscia.com/?p=2613)
⭐ 26 💬 7 👤 mikk14
[🔗 Discuss on HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50084790)

Researchers used a large dataset of Latin inscriptions to estimate the economic complexity of the Western Roman Empire by identifying the professions recorded on funerary and honorific texts and mapping them to provinces. They corrected for biases such as status exaggeration, underrepresentation of women and farmers, and uneven archaeological recovery. ### 6. [Two-Stack Sliding-Window Aggregation](https://orlp.net/blog/two-stack-sliding-window-aggregation/)
⭐ 33 💬 1 👤 surprisetalk
[🔗 Discuss on HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50072485)

The article explains how to maintain sliding-window aggregates efficiently when the summary operation has no inverse, such as minimum, quantiles, or approximate unique counts. It presents a simple two-stack algorithm that achieves amortized constant-time updates for a broad class of associative aggregations by abstracting them into empty, unit, combine, and finalize functions. The post also traces the algorithm’s origins to earlier queue implementations and a more advanced low-latency paper.

### 7. [RPKI now protects 70% of internet routes](https://ipinfo.io/blog/rpki-protects-70-internet-routes)
⭐ 168 💬 57 👤 emot
[🔗 Discuss on HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50112330)

RPKI, which lets IP address holders sign which networks may announce their routes, now covers 70.3% of routed prefixes, up sharply from 54% a year earlier, with China accounting for most of the increase. The system is visibly working: invalid BGP routes are seen by only a handful of monitoring points, while valid and unvalidated routes propagate widely. This suggests RPKI is reducing the risk of accidental or malicious route hijacks without adding meaningful instability to Internet routing.

### 9. [Neural networks are clean](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/)
⭐ 705 💬 266 👤 themgt
[🔗 Discuss on HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50105647)

The author argues that neural networks are far more interpretable than previously believed, because apparent polysemanticity and superposition were largely misread as arbitrary concept packing. ### 10. [The Folders Programming Language](https://danieltemkin.com/esolangs/folders)
⭐ 84 💬 11 👤 gurst
[🔗 Discuss on HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50114342)

Folders is an esoteric programming language that represents programs as nested directory structures, using only the containment and alphabetical order of folders rather than file names or text. Inspired by Whitespace, it treats empty folders as code and can store temporary data in folder names, offering a conceptual model of dematerialized code and potentially unlimited storage. An emulator and interpreter allow users to run Folders programs by loading directories and executing commands based on folder counts.
