## Today's Top 10 on Hacker News

### 1. [Nvidia RTX Spark encrypted firmware slows inference speed to 35% on Linux](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/linux-nvidia-rtx-spark-laptop)
⭐ 139 💬 68 👤 novaRom
[🔗 Discuss on HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50362905)

Nvidia RTX Spark laptops can run Linux with GPU acceleration, but a Windows-only encrypted power-management component limits the GPU to about 780 MHz when plugged in, cutting AI inference speed to roughly 35 percent of Windows performance. The slowdown stems from firmware that Linux cannot read, leaving WSL as the practical workaround until Nvidia ports the power-management logic to its Linux driver.

### 8. [Stringman, an $800 open-source room scale gripper robot](https://neufangled.com/)
⭐ 632 💬 208 👤 nharada
[🔗 Discuss on HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50354358)

Stringman is an open-source, cable-driven robot that uses four ceiling-mounted motors to move a gripper throughout a room, reaching areas inaccessible to wheeled or legged robots. It can carry about 1.9 kg on average and is designed for local, air-gapped control, with published CAD, PCB, and software files. The system is intended as a courier for home labs, allowing other robots and workstations to share parts and objects across the room.

### 10. [The lost malloc()](https://napcakes.nekoweb.org/posts/malloc/)
⭐ 63 💬 20 👤 matt_d
[🔗 Discuss on HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50362466)

This article explores early Unix memory management by examining the original V4 source code, highlighting how a simple linear allocator in the C compiler used sbrk to grow the heap and avoided NULL checks. It also describes the original malloc and mfree functions, which took a map argument to track allocations in core or swap, making them historical precursors to modern malloc.
